PROPOSED SOLUTION - SIH SUBMISSION ANSWERS
Freight Forecasting & Chartering Platform
Bulk cargo procurement into India's East Coast ports
==========================================================================


1. DETAILED EXPLANATION OF THE PROPOSED SOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A constraint-aware decision-support platform that moves chartering from
reactive daily market exploration to a proactive, measured strategy. It is a
working prototype: a FastAPI backend (~10,900 lines of Python, 122 passing
tests), a React dashboard of six screens, and a live ingestion layer pulling
five free public sources daily.

Five engines, each answering one operational question:

  (a) Loadable-Tonnes Engine - deterministic ship physics. Given a vessel,
      berth, tide, water density and coal grade, it computes exactly how many
      tonnes can be loaded, and reports which of four limits bound: draft,
      deadweight, hold volume (low-CV coal cubes out before the marks), or a
      hard dimensional rejection on LOA/beam/air-draft. Auditable against a
      loading computer - nothing here is learned or forecast.

  (b) Chartering Advisor - ranks every feasible way to move a parcel across
      8 vessel classes, 14 ports and 26 berths, with the constraint arithmetic
      shown. Transhipment/lightering is a first-class routing, not an
      exception, because for Haldia (8.5 m draft, 230 m LOA) it usually IS the
      answer - Sandheads has 40-50 m of water a short distance away.

  (c) Freight Forecaster - LightGBM quantile regression producing a calibrated
      predictive distribution (7 quantiles x 6 horizons: 1/2/4/8/13/26 weeks),
      conformalised so the intervals mean what they say, with a SARIMAX second
      opinion, GARCH(1,1) + HAR-RV volatility, and Markov-switching + CUSUM
      regime detection.

  (d) Entry-Timing Optimiser - answers WHEN to fix, not just what the rate
      will be. Fixing is an optimal-stopping problem solved by backward
      induction over correlated scenarios against E[cost] + lambda*CVaR95[cost].
      Returns FIX NOW / WAIT / SPLIT with the value of waiting, the probability
      a later date is cheaper, the regret if the call is wrong, and a
      spot-versus-term efficient frontier.

  (e) Risk Watch - seven monitors (freight volatility, regime shift, port
      congestion, predicted detention, import-plant coal stocks, Bay of Bengal
      cyclones, feed health). Every alert carries the numbers behind it AND
      what it implies for a chartering decision.

Underpinning all of it: a discrete-time hazard model of pre-berthing detention
fitted on 418 reconstructed Paradip port calls (19% censored), which prices the
demurrage term that is often larger than the freight difference between two
routings.


2. HOW IT ADDRESSES THE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mapped directly onto the four deliverables in the problem statement:

  (a) Optimal Market Entry Timing -> opt/timing.py. Not a fan chart but an
      optimal-stopping solution with an explicit carry cost (inventory carry
      plus a stockout penalty scaled by the CEA days-of-burn signal). Without
      that carry term a model always waits, because optionality is free if
      delay costs nothing - the exact mistake a naive "wait for a better rate"
      heuristic makes.

  (b) Vessel Type Optimization -> domain/loadable_tonnes.py + feasibility.py.
      Every recommendation is gated by real berth limits before any economics
      are computed. Draft, LOA, beam, air draft, handling rate and water
      density are read from a versioned constraint master covering all eight
      East Coast ports (Paradip, Vizag, Gangavaram, Gopalpur, Dhamra, Haldia,
      Sagar, Sandheads) plus load ports in Australia, Indonesia, Mozambique
      and the US.

  (c) Idle Scenario Management -> the waiting-time hazard model turns queue
      length into a distribution of days at anchorage, and the timing engine
      prices that as demurrage. Laycan shifting is recommended with the dollar
      figure attached. Transhipment is evaluated as a live alternative to
      idling on a draft-limited berth.

  (d) Risk Mitigation -> domain/alerts.py, seven rules, each a pure function
      over held data, each with severity, observed numbers, source evidence
      and a chartering action. Alerts are persisted rather than recomputed, so
      an alert that fired and later cleared is a testable prediction and rule
      precision becomes measurable after a season.

The stated objective - moving from multiple single spot fixtures to short and
medium term multi-voyage contracts - is answered directly by the contract-mix
efficient frontier, which prices the premium paid for reduced dispersion and
selects the term share that minimises the same risk-adjusted objective.


3. INNOVATION AND UNIQUENESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Physics before prediction. Most freight-forecasting projects predict a
    rate and stop. Here a deterministic, auditable constraint engine gates
    everything, so no recommendation can name a vessel that cannot physically
    berth. The provably-right part carries the provably-uncertain part.

  * An honesty gate that can say "no edge". Dry bulk freight is near a random
    walk in log space, which is why most such projects quietly stop reporting
    skill numbers. Every forecast is scored on strictly walk-forward folds with
    an embargo equal to the horizon, against random-walk and seasonal-naive
    baselines, on BOTH distributional (CRPS) and point (MAE) comparisons. It
    may claim an edge only when it wins both, against every baseline, with
    enough folds. Where it does not, the UI shows a "no edge" badge and the
    timing engine refuses to time the market - returning the real feasibility
    and cost arithmetic instead. An unmeasured model is treated as having no
    edge, not as fine.

  * Calibrated intervals, not decorative ones. Conformalised quantile
    regression (CQR) gives distribution-free, finite-sample coverage. This
    matters because the optimiser takes a CVaR over the tail: if the 90th
    percentile is really the 75th, every downstream number is wrong in the
    direction that makes the platform look more confident than it is.

  * Timing as optimal stopping, not as forecasting. The value of waiting is an
    option value - larger under high volatility, smaller near a deadline,
    smaller again when our own coal stocks are running down.

  * Correlated scenarios. A Gaussian copula ties freight, congestion and
    bunker shocks together, because in a firm market all three move up at once.
    Independent draws let good and bad news cancel and produce a CVaR far too
    flattering to trust.

  * Censoring handled correctly. 19% of port calls are censored; a plain
    regression on waiting days is biased short in all three of the usual
    workarounds. A discrete-time hazard model uses those rows for exactly what
    they tell us and no more.

  * Enforced data provenance. Every ingested row carries source_name,
    source_url, retrieved_at and is_synthetic. The ingestion layer REFUSES to
    write a synthetic row whose source is not declared in SYNTHETIC_DATA.md,
    and a test asserts the disclosure cannot drift out of date. Three badge
    types on screen - real, synthetic, modelled - and model output deliberately
    carries no provenance mixin, so a forecast can never inflate the "% real"
    figure. The FFA forward curve is exchange-licensed and not held, so the
    platform reports "benchmark unavailable" rather than manufacturing one to
    beat; app/ml/evaluate.py enforces that in code.

  * Built entirely on free, public, legally accessible sources. No paid
    Baltic, Kpler, Argus or Clarksons feed is required to run it.


4. TECHNOLOGIES TO BE USED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Backend / API
  Python 3.11, FastAPI, Uvicorn, Pydantic + pydantic-settings, SQLAlchemy,
  SQLite (warehouse.db), DuckDB + PyArrow/Parquet for analytics, PyYAML for
  the versioned constraint masters.

Machine learning / statistics
  LightGBM (quantile objective, direct multi-horizon), scikit-learn (logistic
  hazard), statsmodels (SARIMAX, Markov regime switching), arch (GARCH(1,1),
  Student-t errors), NumPy / SciPy / pandas. Split-conformal calibration
  implemented in-house (app/ml/conformal.py).

Optimisation
  Backward-induction optimal stopping and Gaussian-copula scenario generation
  written in NumPy; Pyomo + HiGHS declared for the planned stochastic MILP.

Ingestion
  httpx, BeautifulSoup4 + lxml, pdfplumber (including a word-coordinate parser
  for stacked PDF table cells), openpyxl / xlrd, APScheduler, websockets (AIS).

Frontend
  React 18 + TypeScript, Vite 6, Apache ECharts (echarts-for-react).
  Six screens: Chartering Advisor, Entry Timing, Risk Watch, Port Watch,
  Market Cockpit, Data Provenance.

Testing / tooling
  pytest (122 tests, including dedicated leakage, conformal-coverage, timing,
  waiting-model and alert-rule suites), Git.

Hardware
  None special. Runs on a single commodity laptop or VM - no GPU, no cluster.
  Models are deliberately small because the histories are short.

Data sources (all free and public, all live)
  Paradip Port Authority daily traffic PDF (queue, berth, LOA, draft);
  CEA daily coal report (stock and days-of-burn for 191 plants, 15 of them
  import-designed); World Bank Pink Sheet (coal, crude, iron ore, monthly);
  Shanghai Shipping Exchange CDFI (route-level coal freight in $/tonne - the
  forecast target); NOAA IBTrACS North Indian Ocean (cyclone best tracks).


5. METHODOLOGY AND PROCESS FOR IMPLEMENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Daily pipeline (scripts/backfill.py, then scripts/run_models.py):

  INGEST      one adapter per source behind a caching base class; every raw
              PDF/XLSX archived byte-for-byte to data/raw/ so a parser
              improvement can be replayed against history rather than
              re-scraped. Provenance stamped on write; undisclosed synthetic
              rows rejected.
                  |
  PANEL       daily causal panel assembled; every column lagged so a row
              dated t is knowable on the morning of t.
                  |
  FEATURES    momentum and realised volatility at 5/10/20/60d; cross-route
              Cape/Panamax/Supramax spreads (the substitution signal); CEA
              days-of-burn level and slope; Paradip queue length, slope and
              60d z-score; coal price and the NEWC-Richards Bay arbitrage
              spread; Fourier annual terms plus monsoon and cyclone-season
              flags. Missing data left missing - LightGBM splits on it.
                  |
  FIT         one model per (horizon, quantile) on the log level. Most recent
              25% held out as a BLOCK - never a random split, since
              neighbouring days in a near-unit-root series are almost the same
              observation - for conformal calibration. Refuses to fit below
              250 usable rows rather than returning a confident-looking curve
              fitted on nothing.
                  |
  CALIBRATE   CQR widening from the held-out fold; quantile crossing repaired
              by sorting.
                  |
  EVALUATE    walk-forward folds with an embargo equal to the horizon; pinball
              loss, CRPS, MASE, empirical 80% coverage, PIT histogram; skill
              against every baseline. Sets the has_edge flag consumed
              downstream.
                  |
  SIDE MODELS GARCH(1,1) + HAR-RV volatility; Markov-switching + CUSUM regime;
              discrete-time berthing hazard from the port-call archive.
                  |
  DECIDE      feasibility screen -> correlated scenario draws through the
              calibrated quantiles -> backward induction

                  V(T) = fix_cost(T)                               (deadline)
                  V(d) = min( fix_cost(d), E[V(d+1)] + carry(d) )  (earlier)

              on E[cost] + lambda*CVaR95[cost] -> contract-mix frontier.
                  |
  ALERT       seven pure-function rules over held data; raise / refresh / clear
              lifecycle handled idempotently.
                  |
  SERVE       19 REST endpoints; React dashboard.

Repository layout
  backend/app/ingest/   one adapter per source, caching base class
  backend/app/domain/   loadable_tonnes.py (physics), feasibility.py (screen),
                        alerts.py (risk rules)
  backend/app/ml/       panel, features, quantile, conformal, volatility,

  backend/app/opt/      scenarios.py (correlated draws), timing.py (stopping)

6. POTENTIAL IMPACT ON THE TARGET AUDIENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary users - chartering and logistics managers procuring imported coal for
East Coast Indian ports: utilities, steel and cement producers, port operators
and government importers.

  * Replaces daily broker-dependent market exploration with a standing,
    evidence-backed recommendation, reclaiming analyst hours currently spent
    reconstructing the same picture every morning.

  * Makes the decision auditable. Every recommendation shows the constraint
    arithmetic, the source of every number, and the cost of being wrong - so a
    fixture can be justified to a procurement committee or an auditor rather
    than defended as judgement.

  * Prevents the expensive, invisible errors: nominating a vessel that cannot
    berth at its declared draft, under-loading because low-CV coal cubed out,
    or discovering a lightering requirement after the fixture is signed.

  * Institutionalises knowledge. Berth limits, tidal behaviour, transhipment
    topology and demurrage patterns currently live in a few experienced heads;
    here they are versioned files and tested code that survive staff turnover.

  * Shifts the negotiating position. Walking into a fixture with a calibrated
    distribution, a quantified value of waiting and a term-versus-spot frontier
    is a materially stronger stance than reacting to a broker's quote.

  * Builds trust by refusing to overclaim. The "no edge" badge means users
    learn precisely when to rely on the model and when to rely on judgement.
    The failure mode that kills adoption of forecasting tools is a confident
    wrong number, and this system is engineered not to produce one.


7. BENEFITS OF THE SOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Economic
  * Lower delivered cost per tonne through better fixture timing, and by
    trading spot exposure against term cover on a priced frontier rather than
    by habit.
  * Reduced demurrage and detention: pre-berthing waiting time is forecast as
    a distribution and priced, and laycans can be shifted with a dollar figure
    attached. On draft-constrained lanes this term frequently exceeds the
    freight difference between two routings.
  * Higher vessel utilisation: loading to the true binding constraint rather
    than to a conservative rule of thumb means fewer tonnes left behind on
    every voyage.
  * Fewer idle days and less deadheading through demand-aware positioning.
  * Zero recurring data cost - the entire stack runs on free public sources,
    so it is deployable without a Baltic or Kpler subscription.

Operational and supply-chain
  * More predictable coal availability at import-designed plants, driven by the
    CEA days-of-burn signal feeding the urgency term directly.
  * Earlier warning of congestion, cyclones, regime shifts and feed failures,
    with a stated action attached to each.
  * A defensible, reproducible audit trail for every chartering decision.

Environmental
  * Better-utilised vessels mean fewer voyages per tonne delivered, and lower
    fuel burn and CO2 per tonne-mile.
  * Less time idling at anchorage - auxiliary engines running at anchor burn
    fuel and emit inside coastal airsheds near dense population centres.
  * Reduced deadheading removes ballast voyages that carry no cargo at all.

Social and institutional
  * More reliable fuel supply to coastal thermal plants supports grid stability
    for the populations they serve.
  * Reduced landed cost of imported coal feeds through to power generation
    cost.
  * Lower cyclone exposure for crews and port workers, through explicit Bay of
    Bengal seasonal risk in the timing decision.
  * The provenance and honesty architecture is itself reusable: any Indian
    public-sector analytics programme facing the same "can we trust this
    number" question can adopt the pattern.
